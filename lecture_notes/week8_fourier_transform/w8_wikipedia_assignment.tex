\section{Wikipedia assignment: Explore, research, and contribute}
This assignment connects EE 102 with applications, people, instruments, and
ideas beyond our lectures. Your team will learn about a signal-processing
topic, research published sources, and contribute a clear, well-supported
writeup to its Wikipedia article. You are writing for readers outside this
class and helping them understand something you have learned.

\textbf{This is part of Midterm \#3:} the Wikipedia assignment contributes
\textbf{20 points}, and the written exam contributes \textbf{80 points}.
Teams have already been assigned randomly in CatCourses.

\subsection{What the training prepares you to do}
The starter module introduces Wikipedia's core policies, copyright and
plagiarism, and what makes a topic notable. Further training supports
finding reliable sources, evaluating articles, adding citations, editing
and drafting in a sandbox, and working with other editors.
Use the dashboard's timeline to see the training assigned to our course.

Explain reliable published sources in your own words, with citations and
a neutral tone. Research means learning from the literature; it does not
mean publishing original scientific claims.

\textbf{Wiki Education's AI guidance:} Write your contribution yourself.
Do not use AI tools to write or edit your Wikipedia text.
See Wiki Education's
\href{https://wikiedu.org/teach-with-wikipedia/}{assignment overview}
and
\href{https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f3/Editing_Wikipedia_brochure_(Wiki_Education_Foundation)_(2023).pdf}{student editing guide}
are useful companions to the dashboard training.

\subsection{Topics and article directions for EE 102}
You can explore topics we have not yet studied in detail. Look for a clear
connection to signals, systems, frequency content, or the history of these
ideas. Some directions to investigate are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Audio and music:} pitch, timbre, noise reduction, or the
    \href{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_vocoder}{phase vocoder}.
    Research how time- and frequency-domain descriptions support a specific
    audio technique or application.
    \item \textbf{Scientific instruments:} the
    \href{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lock-in_amplifier}{lock-in amplifier},
    oscilloscopes, or spectrum analyzers. Explore how an instrument extracts
    useful information from a measured signal.
    \item \textbf{Signals in other fields:} EEG/ECG recordings, seismic
    signals, or sonar. A focused application can connect our mathematical
    tools to measurements and questions in another discipline.
    \item \textbf{History and people:} the development of harmonic analysis,
    early sound-analysis instruments, or a documented contribution to
    signal-processing history. Research the context and evidence behind
    the development, as well as the technical idea.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Choose a contribution you can support}
Read a candidate article and its references. Identify a specific section
or explanation your team could improve, then look for reliable published
sources that support that improvement. A focused section about an
application, historical development, or technical explanation is a
manageable starting point. Existing articles can offer useful opportunities
for expansion, clearer explanations, or stronger citations.

\textbf{Discuss with your team:} What would you like to learn? What is the
connection to EE 102? What could a reader understand better after your
contribution? What published sources will help you explain it accurately?
