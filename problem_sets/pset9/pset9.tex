\problem{1} Pulsating signals are all around us! The frequency domain representation of a pulse signal is quite useful. So, in this problem, consider an aperiodic pulse given below and answer the questions that follow
\[
p(t)=
\begin{cases}
2,&-1<t<1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

\problempart[30 points] Sketch $p(t)$. Before calculating its transform,
use its symmetry to predict whether $P(\omega)$ is real and whether it is
even. Then derive $P(\omega)$ and give its value at $\omega=0$.


\problempart[40 points] Let $q(t)=p(t-2)$. Sketch $q(t)$ and obtain
$Q(\omega)$ using the time-shifting property (see week 9 lecture 1). Explain why
$|Q(\omega)|=|P(\omega)|$ even though $q(t)$ is not even about $t=0$.


\problempart[30 points] Now let $r(t)=p(t-2)+p(t+2)$. Without performing
another transform integral, find $R(\omega)$ and write it as a real,
even expression. Evaluate $R(0)$ and $R(\pi/4)$. Explain how the two
shifted pulses can cancel at one frequency even though both pulses
are positive in time.
