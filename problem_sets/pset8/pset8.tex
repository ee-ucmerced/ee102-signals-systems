Use the following Fourier transform convention
\[
X(\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt,
\qquad
x(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}X(\omega)e^{j\omega t}\,d\omega.
\]

\problem{1} Frequency content of signals. 

Frequency content of signals is often the property that engineers look for when analyzing systems. In this problem, we will analyze the frequency content of signals.

\problempart[5 points] A signal is written as
\[
z(t)=3e^{j2t}+3e^{-j2t}.
\]
Express it using sinusoids, identify its amplitude, fundamental angular frequency, and describe its frequency content.

Hint: $e^{j\theta} = \cos\theta + j\sin\theta$.

\problempart[5 points] Sketch
\[
p(t)=u(t)-u(t-1),\qquad q(t)=1+\cos(2t).
\]

What are the frequency contents of each of the signals? A descriptive answer is fine.

\problem{2} Application example.

Consider a series RC circuit with $R=2\,\mathrm{k}\Omega$ and
$C=250\,\mu\mathrm F$. Measure the output voltage across the capacitor.
Assume zero initial capacitor voltage. You may use
\[
\tau_C=RC,\qquad h_C(t)=\frac{1}{\tau_C}e^{-t/\tau_C}u(t),
\qquad
y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\lambda)h_C(t-\lambda)\,d\lambda.
\]

\problempart[5 points] Calculate $\tau_C$ and write $h_C(t)$ with numerical
constants. Find the output for $x(t)=3\delta(t-\tfrac12)$.
When does the response begin?

Hint: $x(t)*A\delta(t-t_0)=A x(t-t_0)$.

\problempart[10 points] For the same circuit with $x(t)=6u(t)$ volts, evaluate
the convolution integral to find the capacitor voltage for all $t$.
Find the voltage at $t=0^+$, $t=0.5$, $t=1$, and as $t\to\infty$.



\problem{3} Practice Fourier Series.

\problempart[5 points] For
\[
r(t)=1+4\cos(6t)-2\sin(10t),
\]
find the fundamental angular frequency and period, and list every nonzero
complex Fourier coefficient, including the DC term.

Hints: Find a common fundamental frequency for 6 and 10 rad/s. Then use
\[
\cos\theta=\frac{e^{j\theta}+e^{-j\theta}}{2},
\qquad
\sin\theta=\frac{e^{j\theta}-e^{-j\theta}}{2j}.
\]



\problempart[5 points] 
A signal generator produces the periodic voltage $v(t+3)=v(t)$.
Over $-3/2\le t<3/2$, define
\[
v(t)=
\begin{cases}
6,& |t|<1/2,\\
0,& 1/2<|t|<3/2.
\end{cases}
\]

Sketch two periods. Find $T$, $f_0$, and $\omega_0$,
and identify what fraction of each period the voltage is nonzero.

Hint: The pulse has width 1 second and repeats every 3 seconds. Break down the integral accordingly.

\problempart[10 points] For the signal in (b), derive $a_0$ and $a_k$ for $k\ne0$ using the
Fourier series analysis equation. Identify the nonzero indices for which
$a_k=0$ and explain the meaning of $a_0$ for this voltage.
\[
a_k=\frac{1}{T}\int_{-T/2}^{T/2}v(t)e^{-jk\omega_0t}\,dt.
\]

\problem{4} Energy content of signals.
% Adapted from Fall 2025 HW8 Problem 2.

A signal generator can produce the following voltages, all with period
$T=2$ seconds and peak magnitude 3:
\[
s(t)=3\cos(\pi t),\qquad
b(t)=
\begin{cases}3,&0\le t<1,\\-3,&1\le t<2,\end{cases}
\qquad b(t+2)=b(t),
\]
and the sawtooth $r(t)=3t$ for $-1\le t<1$, with $r(t+2)=r(t)$.
For this problem, the coefficients are supplied so you can concentrate on
their energy interpretation:
\[
\begin{aligned}
&s:\quad c_{1}=c_{-1}=\tfrac32,\quad c_k=0\text{ otherwise},\\
&b:\quad d_0=0,\quad
d_k=\begin{cases}\dfrac{6}{j\pi k},&k\text{ odd},\\[3pt]0,&k\ne0\text{ even},\end{cases}\\
&r:\quad e_0=0,\quad e_k=\dfrac{3j(-1)^k}{\pi k}\quad(k\ne0).
\end{aligned}
\]

\problempart[5 points] Use Parseval's theorem to calculate the average
energy over a period (power) of each voltage. Give numerical exact values.
\[
P_x=\frac{1}{T}\int_{t_0}^{t_0+T}|x(t)|^2\,dt
=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}|a_k|^2.
\]
Hints: Include both signs of the index. You may use
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^2}=\frac{\pi^2}{6},
\qquad
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2m+1)^2}=\frac{\pi^2}{8}.
\]

\problempart[3 points] Assuming other things are same, rank the three voltages by the average energy over a period.
Then calculate the energy over one period for each.

Hint: $E_{x,T}=T P_x$. For this part, you may use
$P_s=9/2$, $P_b=9$, and $P_r=3$ independently of the preceding sum.

\problempart[4 points] For each waveform, calculate the fundamental pair's
contribution $P_{\mathrm{fund}}=|a_1|^2+|a_{-1}|^2$ to $P_x$, and the
fraction $P_{\mathrm{fund}}/P_x$. Use the corresponding supplied coefficients
$c_k$, $d_k$, or $e_k$ in place of $a_k$.

\problempart[3 points] Which waveform has the greatest total average
energy over a period? Which has the greatest fraction in its fundamental?
Use these results to explain why the waveform with the most total energy
need not be the closest to a single sinusoid.


\problem{5} Standard Fourier transforms.

\problempart[10 points] A voltage pulse is
\[
p(t)=3[u(t+1)-u(t-1)].
\]
Sketch it and derive $P(\omega)$ directly from the transform integral.
Find $P(0)$ and the first positive zero. Evaluate $P(\pi/2)$.
Explain what the zero-frequency value tells you about the pulse.

Hints: Integrate over $-1<t<1$. You may write your answer using
$\operatorname{sinc}(z)=\sin z/z$, with $\operatorname{sinc}(0)=1$.
At $\omega=0$, use the signed area of the pulse.

\problempart[5 points] Find the transform of
\[
q(t)=2\delta(t-1)-\delta(t+1).
\]
Evaluate $Q(0)$ and $Q(\pi/2)$. Give their magnitudes and phases,
and explain how the shifted impulses affect the frequency-domain result.

Hint: Sifting gives
$\int f(t)\delta(t-t_0)\,dt=f(t_0)$, with the integral over all time.
You may use $e^{\pm j\pi/2}=\pm j$.

\problempart[5 points] Use the inverse transform integral and sifting
to reconstruct the real signal corresponding to
\[
X(\omega)=4\pi\delta(\omega)
+\pi\delta(\omega-2)+\pi\delta(\omega+2).
\]
Hint: Each impulse contributes its weight times
$e^{j\omega t}/(2\pi)$ at the impulse's frequency. Combine the two
nonzero-frequency terms using Euler's formula.


\problem{6} Frequency content of an RC circuit

% Extends the RC-transform practice in Fall 2025 HW8 Problem 3.
A voltage measurement circuit uses a series RC network with output across
the capacitor. Here $R=1\,\mathrm{k}\Omega$, $C=250\,\mu\mathrm F$,
and the initially relaxed circuit has impulse response
\[
h(t)=4e^{-4t}u(t).
\]

\problempart[8 points] Derive $H(\omega)$ by applying the Fourier
transform to $h(t)$. Find $H(0)$ and the magnitude and phase at
$\omega=4$. Describe what happens to the magnitude as $|\omega|$ grows.


\problempart[8 points] Now, consider two impulses applied to the circuit. So, we have
\[
x(t)=\delta(t)+\delta(t-\tfrac12).
\]
Find the output $y(t)$ using LTI properties. Then obtain $Y(\omega)$
from the output signal using linearity and a change of variables in the
delayed term. 

\problempart[4 points] If the circuit has double the capacitance, that is,  $C = 500\,\mu\mathrm F$.
Then, find the the DC gain and the magnitude of the
frequency response at $\omega=4$. Which circuit reduces a component at
4 rad/s more? Explain what happens to the charging timescale when
the capacitance doubles.
